### Source resilience

The IGC page now exceeds 1 MB and responds slowly during earthquake sequences. The adapter reads only its first table (about a week of events) and stops the download there. IGC, EMSC and GDACS adapters keep their last successful response for up to 30 minutes when a refresh fails; it is returned with `stale: true` and its age, so the browser shows it as older data with its original check time. When the selected catalogue fails, the activity screen offers the other catalogues in one tap.

## Local assistance

The Panama mission directory in `src/data/consularMissions.ts` contains 24 records with current official source links and 26 historical references from the EU's October 2024 directory. Not every current-source record has an address or phone. Historical references are collapsed, dated, and never used for phone calls, routing or map pins. This is not an exhaustive diplomatic registry. An unsupported combination means Faro has no record, not that the country lacks representation. Seven building markers have documented coordinates; Spain's marker and directions are withheld while a temporary move remains unconfirmed.

Nearby pharmacies and hospitals are explicit Google Maps searches, not live availability data. The user enters a place or requests approximate GPS coordinates; the query is sent only when they open a search. Official complementary sources are [MINSA's facilities directory](https://instalaciones.minsa.gob.pa/) and [SENAPRED's Visor Preparado](https://www.senapred.gov.cl/visor-preparado/).

The emergency checklist is based on [SENAPRED's emergency kit](https://www.senapred.gov.cl/kit-de-emergencia/) and [family preparation](https://senapred.cl/familia-preparada/), with [Ready.gov's kit](https://www.ready.gov/kit) for the second water reference. It has 14 base items plus two that appear only for the hazards the person selects: an N95 mask and goggles (earthquake, volcano) and a waterproof bag (hurricane, flood). A power bank is an explicit Faro addition. The kit calculates water for the chosen people and days with both references (SENAPRED 2 L per person per day; Ready.gov about 3.8 L, which also covers hygiene) and groups unchecked items by the kind of shop that sells them, each with a Google Maps search the person opens. Checklist marks and the plan stay in the browser. Earthquake protection uses unmodified ECA / ShakeOut illustrations with attribution. The other topics use original Faro diagrams (cross-sections, floor plans and an NHC timeline) whose labels repeat the written steps; they are not SENAPRED illustrations. See THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md for usage terms.

## Official response summaries

`src/data/responses.ts` holds hand-reviewed summaries of an official response to a specific disaster, shown on the situation screen for that country. Each fact names its source and the time it refers to; links go only to the publishing agencies. A summary has a `reviewedAt` date and stops showing after `showUntil` unless it is reviewed again. The current entry covers the 9 October 2026 M7.7 earthquake in Panama: [SINAPROC statement #8](https://www.sinaproc.gob.pa/comunicado-oficial-8/) (figures to 11:30 on 12 October), the [U.S. Department of State response page](https://www.state.gov/responding-to-panama-earthquake/) (consular line, STEP and donation guidance), the [USGS event page](https://earthquake.usgs.gov/earthquakes/eventpage/us6000u18k), [Copernicus EMS activation EMSR934](https://mapping.emergency.copernicus.eu/activations/EMSR934/) and the [FTC guide to donating safely](https://consumer.ftc.gov/features/donating-safely-and-avoiding-scams). The card shows each agency's magnitude for the event (7.7 per USGS, 7.2 per IGC, both checked on 2026-10-14), because agencies compute it differently. The U.S. consular line is shown only to people who selected U.S. nationality. Faro does not label later earthquakes as aftershocks and does not describe shelters as safe.

The emergency-number directory covers Panama, Chile, Colombia, Argentina, the United States, Mexico, Peru, Ecuador and Costa Rica with explicit source and geographic caveats. No global default number is invented. Some numbers apply only to a specific domestic jurisdiction, including SAMU coverage in Peru. See [country coverage](country-coverage.md) and the country modules in `src/data/destinations/`.

Guidance links to SENAPRED, CDC, NOAA and national/local authorities. Chilean evacuation maps apply to Chile; they do not identify safe locations in Panama. Institutions and volunteer links do not imply facilities are open or available.

## Updating records

Record an official source, applicable country/jurisdiction, review date and uncertainty. Retain source-issued dates separately from fetch time. Use synthetic test fixtures; do not commit a live cache as application data. A missing/failed source must remain visibly missing/failed. Have relevant authorities or qualified local operators validate safety-critical wording before deployment.

## Tsunami bulletin status

The international NOAA/PTWC products Faro checks are not local alerts. Faro distinguishes informational statements, explicit ends of threat or cancellations, and records superseded by a later bulletin for the same event and basin. It never infers an end of threat from conditional text or from a cancellation limited to another coast.

The six-hour window only organizes the display; it is not an official expiry. Earlier messages appear in a collapsed archive with their issue time and bulletin status. Recent threat messages are shown as unconfirmed and link to the official update. A failed or partial check, or one older than two minutes, does not confirm whether a message is still in effect. The archive holds the latest products received, not the full sequence of each event.

Sources: [NOAA products](https://www.tsunami.gov/?page=product_list) and [how alerts are updated and cancelled](https://www.weather.gov/safety/tsunami-alerts).
